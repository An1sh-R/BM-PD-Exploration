EXTRA SCANS: REPORT
2026-10-25

The async scan (A <= 1, no WS, f down to 0.1) showed that the fixed-partner and structure
effects came from players updating in lockstep. The extra scans close its three gaps:
fully sequential updating (one player at a time), regime A > 1, and clustering (WS - ER).
Result: without lockstep only degrees have a consistent effect. Hubs defect at A <= 1
under every update scheme (the Facebook exception was lockstep too). Fixed partners are
neutral at beta = 1 and lower cooperation at high beta. At A > 1 fixed seats and uneven
degrees lower cooperation and the hub reversal gets stronger. The high-beta clustering
benefit was lockstep.

Every number below was recomputed from results/extra_regimes_runs.csv and
results/extra_sequential_runs.csv (and the async scan for the A <= 1 ER and f = 1, 0.1 runs).


1. WHAT WAS RUN
---------------

Mentor's script (src/extra_scans.py, merged 2026-10-24).

--regimes: the async-scan design (update fraction f = 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1; 1,500/f rounds,
window 500/f, so every player gets about 1,500 updates at every f) for
  - regime A > 1 (a cooperator can never be satisfied): b {1.7, 2.0} x a {0.6, 0.7},
    graph types real, degseq, samedeg, shuffled, ER, WS;
  - WS in the async scan's A <= 1 cells (b {1.5, 2.0} x a {0.2, 0.4}), paired with the
    async scan's ER runs: clustering = WS - ER.
  10 networks x beta {1, 10, 100} x 5 reps = 16,800 runs.

--sequential: one player at a time (fast_model.run_bm_sequential). Each update: a random
player and its partners act, the player gets its average payoff and updates; nobody else
changes in between. 1,500 sweeps (about 1,500 updates per player), last 500 recorded.
This is the f -> 0 limit. 8 networks (Deezer and GitHub too slow one at a time), 6 graph
types, beta {1, 100}, cells (b, a) = (1.5, 0.4), (2.0, 0.2) [A <= 1] and (2.0, 0.7)
[A > 1], 3 reps = 864 runs. The analysis compares each contrast with its f = 1 and
f = 0.1 values on the same cells and reps.

Checks:
  - Before the runs: 22/22 unit tests; numpy vs Mesa 13/13 cells (output unchanged);
    one at a time vs f = 0.05 at equal updates per player: 6/6 cells agree,
    |difference| <= 0.0007 (emaileu, 3 seatings, beta 1 and 100, 8 seeds).
  - f = 1 equals the synchronous sweeps bit-identically: 4,200 of 4,200 runs.
  - All 16,800 + 864 runs present, no missing values. |drift| > 0.01 in at most 0.07% of
    runs (none one at a time). Contrast 95% CIs <= 0.010 (regimes), <= 0.005 (one at a
    time, 3 reps).

Known difference from the f -> 0 limit: in the one-at-a-time shuffled and samedeg
versions, partners are drawn independently, so a player can meet the same partner twice
or itself (f < 1 uses a permutation). Small here: the f = 0.05 check passes.

Mean cooperation, real graphs, A > 1 (f = 1 -> 0.1): 0.442 -> 0.423 at beta = 1,
0.479 -> 0.429 at beta = 10, 0.476 -> 0.430 at beta = 100. As at A <= 1, the rise with
beta is mostly lockstep.


2. H10: PREDICTION VS OUTCOME
-----------------------------

H10 was written before the runs (ResearchQ/question.md).

(a) One at a time: every contrast within 0.005 of f = 0.1.
    PARTLY FAILS. Within 0.005 for degree spread, keeping one's degree, clustering
    (max 0.007) and structure beyond degree (max 0.006). But fixed partners at A <= 1,
    beta = 100 keep falling: 4 of 8 networks more than 0.005 below f = 0.1 (grqc -0.022 ->
    -0.034). The conclusions (a) predicted still hold: fixed partners about 0 at beta = 1
    and negative at beta = 100; hub defection, degree spread and keeping one's degree at
    beta = 1 hold.
(b) A > 1 without lockstep: fixed seats and degree spread negative at beta = 100, hub
    reversal positive, none shrinking as f falls.
    HOLDS, more strongly: all three grow as f falls, on all 10 networks.
(c) Clustering, A <= 1: at beta = 100 shrinks with f, below 0.01 at f = 0.1 on at least
    8 of 10; at beta = 1 within +/-0.01.
    PARTLY. Below 0.01 counting the sign on 9 of 10, by absolute size on 7 of 10 (grqc
    +0.045, Facebook -0.061, asinternet -0.032). beta = 1 within +/-0.01 except asinternet
    (-0.014).

7. CLAIM REVISIONS (applied 2026-10-25 in ResearchQ/question.md and README.md)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

C1  Now: robust to every update scheme tested. Facebook's beta = 100 exception was
    lockstep. A > 1: the reversal is not lockstep (stronger without it; one at a time
    already at beta = 1).
C2  Before: fixed partners help only in lockstep.
    Now: without lockstep they are neutral at beta = 1 and lower cooperation at high beta
    (one at a time -0.007 to -0.034 at beta = 100). At A > 1 fixed seats lower cooperation
    on all 10 networks. Keeping one's degree helps at low beta, robustly.
C3  Before: uneven degrees help, robustly; structure beyond degree barely matters without
    lockstep; clustering untested.
    Now: uneven degrees help at A <= 1 and lower cooperation at A > 1, beta >= 10.
    Structure beyond degree has no consistent effect without lockstep (f = 0.1: -0.032 to
    +0.019; one at a time -0.010 to +0.016), reworded from "barely matters". The
    high-beta clustering benefit was lockstep (grqc residual shrinking).


8. CAVEATS
----------

- One at a time covers 8 networks, 3 cells, beta 1 and 100, 3 reps.
- A > 1 cells are b 1.7/2.0 x a 0.6/0.7 only. At beta = 100 there updates are all or
  nothing (p -> 0 or 1) and a defector needs >= a of its neighbours cooperating, so the
  dynamics are mostly noise-driven; some contrasts are near 0 by construction.
- Means over 4 (b, a) cells can hide state switches in single cells (Facebook and
  asinternet clustering).
- ER, WS are one graph each: CIs hold run noise only. Node-level rho: no CIs per network
  beyond the reps.
- Why fixed partners lower cooperation without lockstep has not been studied.
