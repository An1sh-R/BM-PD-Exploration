# Research Question

## Question

Weak PD (R = 1, T = b, S = P = 0) played by Bush–Mosteller (BM) aspiration learners with averaged payoffs on real networks. How much do (1) a player's degree, (2) fixed partners and (3) network structure beyond the degree sequence change cooperation, and how does each depend on learning sensitivity β and aspiration A = a·b?

"Weak" is the game's name (S = P = 0, the mildest PD), not weak selection.

## Why

- BM = reinforcement learning: players judge their own payoff against an aspiration; nobody copies anyone. Cooperation on networks is mostly studied with imitation on synthetic graphs.
- Ezaki et al. 2016 (lattice): β changes BM cooperation. Ezaki & Masuda 2017 (rings, one β): fixed partners help BM, effect depends on degree k and b/c.
- Earlier work finds RL cooperation largely insensitive to topology (Cimini & Sánchez 2014; Amaral et al. 2016). Here: real networks, node level, across β and A.

## Claims (experiment 1 + extension + desync check + async scan + extra scans: 10 networks, 533,664 runs; numbers in README.md)

Stated with their conditions. First written 2026-10-10 from experiment 1 (5 networks); revised 2026-10-13 after the extension sweep (H1–H7); revised 2026-10-23 after the desynchronised-update check (H8; `report_desync.txt`) and again after the async scan (H9; `report_async.txt`); revised 2026-10-25 after the extra scans (H10; `report_extra.txt`). A ≤ 1 = a cooperator can be satisfied (main regime). "Sync" = all players update every round (the main sweeps); "desync" = each player updates with probability 0.5 per round (84,000 runs, β = 1, 3, 10, 100, a = 0.1–0.7). "Async scan" = update fraction f = 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1 with 1,500/f rounds, so every player gets ~1,500 updates at every f (12,000 runs, A ≤ 1: β = 1, 10, 100, a = 0.2, 0.4, b = 1.5, 2.0, 5 reps; no WS, no A > 1). Effects that shrink as f falls come from everyone updating at the same moment (lockstep), not from the network. "Extra scans" (`src/extra_scans.py`): the async-scan design for A > 1 (b = 1.7, 2.0 × a = 0.6, 0.7; 6 graph types incl. WS) and for WS in the A ≤ 1 cells (16,800 runs); and "one at a time" = fully sequential updating, one player per update (864 runs: 8 networks, no Deezer/GitHub; β = 1, 100; cells (b, a) = (1.5, 0.4), (2.0, 0.2), (2.0, 0.7); 3 reps).

- **C1. Low-degree players cooperate more than hubs. Robust to every update scheme tested.** A ≤ 1, sync: Spearman(node cooperation, degree) < 0 on all 10 networks at every β (−0.11 to −0.77; LastFM: top-1% hubs 0.17 vs degree-1 nodes 0.45). Strongest at β ≈ 1–2, weaker at high β. Exception: Facebook at β ≥ 10 (−0.03 to +0.11). Desync: still negative on all 10 (−0.13 to −0.67) except Facebook at β = 100 (+0.05); typically 0.08 weaker. Async scan, f = 0.1: negative on all 10 at β = 1 (−0.52 to −0.83, 80–94% of its f = 1 size) and β = 10 (−0.36 to −0.70, incl. Facebook); at β = 100 on 9 of 10 (−0.30 to −0.64), Facebook ≈ −0.05 (sync +0.22). One at a time: negative on all 8 at β = 1 (−0.51 to −0.82) and β = 100 (−0.14 to −0.64, incl. Facebook −0.14; sync on the same cells +0.24): the Facebook exception was lockstep.
  - A > 1: reverses (hubs cooperate more) at high β. Sync: first positive at β ≈ 2–20 depending on the network (β 2 emaileu, Facebook; 3 GitHub, Twitch, astroph; 5 Deezer, LastFM, gnutella; 10 asinternet; 20 grqc; up to +0.71), not steady (positive at β 2–3, near 0 around β = 10, rising again). Desync: stronger and steady, positive by β = 3 on 9 of 10 networks (asinternet from β = 10), +0.60 to +0.78 at β = 100. The dip near β = 10 was a lockstep effect. Extra scans, f = 0.1: positive on all 10 at β = 10 (+0.51 to +0.73; sync −0.03 to +0.33) and β = 100 (+0.62 to +0.78; sync +0.10 to +0.68). One at a time (cell (2.0, 0.7)): +0.51 to +0.85 at β = 100 (f = 0.1 on the same cell within 0.02). At β ≥ 10 the reversal is not a lockstep effect; lockstep weakened it. At β = 1 removing lockstep raises ρ too (4-cell median −0.55 sync → −0.07 at f = 0.1; sync negative in all 4 cells on all 10, cell medians −0.68 to −0.39), and whether it ends positive depends on the cell: on (2.0, 0.7) positive on 7 of 8 both one at a time (+0.18 to +0.27) and at f = 0.1 (+0.14 to +0.26; sync −0.48 to −0.13); over the 4 A > 1 cells at f = 0.1 negative on 7 of 10 (median over networks −0.29 at (1.7, 0.6) to +0.18 at (2.0, 0.7)). Possible mechanism (untested): at A > 1 hubs' x stays below a (band test, all A > 1 cells), so a hub is unsatisfied whatever it plays; low-degree players often see x ≥ a (e.g. x = 1), where defecting satisfies, so they drift to D. Gap: x was measured only in sync runs, where it is also below a yet ρ < 0 at β = 1, so this does not explain why the reversal needs asynchronous updating.
  - a = 0: weak, only around β ≈ 1 (≥ −0.32; sync only).
  - Degrees alone produce it: negative on every degree-preserving rewiring, BA (−0.31 to −0.54), ER (−0.18 to −0.64) and WS (weakest, −0.06 to −0.44) at β = 0.1–3, sync (β = 0.01 excluded, reporting rules). Desync at β = 1: degseq −0.51 to −0.69, ER −0.17 to −0.51, WS −0.04 to −0.31. Fits the mechanism below, which uses only a node's degree.
  - At high β (sync) real structure changes its size on some networks (astroph real −0.27 vs degseq −0.01; emaileu −0.11 vs +0.19 at β = 100); on asinternet, gnutella real ≈ degseq. Node level = rep 0 only, no CIs (async scan: 5 reps).
  - Proposed mechanism: with averaged payoffs and a share x of cooperating neighbours, a cooperator earns x (satisfied iff x ≥ a·b), a defector b·x (satisfied iff x ≥ a). A hub sees x ≈ its neighbourhood's mean. If a ≤ x < a·b, cooperating disappoints and defecting satisfies → hubs drift to D. A degree-1 player with one cooperating partner gets 1 ≥ A and stays C. Band test (2026-10-13, `src/band_test.py`, sync): partly supported. Hubs defect most when their x is in the band (9 of 10 networks), but also below and above it, so the band alone does not explain C1.
  - Wording: "in the model, on real network structure". Not a claim about real people.
- **C2. Fixed partners help BM only when players update in lockstep; without it they are neutral at β = 1 and lower cooperation at high β. Keeping one's degree helps at low β, robustly.** Sync, A ≤ 1: real − shuffled +0.006 to +0.040 at β = 0.1 → +0.030 to +0.108 at β = 100. Split by the same-degree shuffle: real − shuffled = (real − samedeg: fixed partners) + (samedeg − shuffled: keeping one's degree).
  - Partners only (real − samedeg). Sync: +0.002 to +0.009 at β = 0.1 → +0.016 to +0.104 at β = 100 on 9 of 10 (emaileu −0.010 to −0.003 at β ≥ 10). Desync: about a third left at β ≥ 10. Async scan: shrinks steadily as f falls, at every β. At f = 0.1: β = 1 at most +0.007 (median 23% of f = 1); β ≥ 10 slightly **negative**, −0.012 to −0.019 on 6 (β = 10) and 7 (β = 100) of 10 networks (CIs exclude 0), the rest −0.007 to +0.004. At f = 0.5 the effect is the same after ~750 and ~1,500 updates per player (≤ 0.006 apart): the drop comes from breaking the lockstep, not from fewer updates. So the growth with β under sync (and the earlier "fixed partners help more at high β") is a lockstep effect. One at a time: β = 1 −0.003 to +0.006; β = 100 negative on all 8 (−0.007 to −0.034), further below f = 0.1 on 4 of 8 by > 0.005 (grqc −0.022 → −0.034). Why it turns negative: not studied.
  - Keeping one's degree (samedeg − shuffled): at low β on very uneven-degree networks most of real − shuffled (β = 0.1 sync: asinternet 0.037 of 0.040, GitHub 0.025 of 0.029, Twitch 0.016 of 0.021). Unchanged at β = 1 by desync and at every f of the async scan (asinternet +0.039, GitHub +0.033, Twitch +0.021, emaileu +0.014, others ≤ +0.008). At β ≥ 10 its sync size (up to +0.093) is lockstep (≤ +0.011 at f = 0.1). One at a time: β = 1 unchanged (asinternet +0.043, Twitch +0.024, emaileu +0.015); β = 100 +0.008 to +0.025 on its 2 A ≤ 1 cells.
  - What is left of real − shuffled at f = 0.1, β = 1 (≥ 0.01 on 6 of 10, up to +0.039) is mostly keeping one's degree.
  - A > 1: fixed seats lower cooperation once the lockstep is gone. real − shuffled at f = 0.1, all 10: β = 1 −0.003 to 0.000, β = 10 −0.006 to −0.031, β = 100 −0.005 to −0.035 (sync +0.001 to +0.027; desync β = 100 −0.003 to −0.020). Both parts are negative at β ≥ 10: fixed partners −0.003 to −0.015, keeping one's degree −0.003 to −0.020. One at a time: same as f = 0.1 (within 0.003).
  - "More than imitators" is not tested here: do not claim it.
- **C3. Uneven degrees help when a cooperator can be satisfied (A ≤ 1) and lower cooperation when it cannot (A > 1, β ≥ 10), robustly. Beyond the degree sequence, structure has no consistent effect once players do not update in lockstep.** Degree spread (degseq − ER): +0.016 to +0.066 on all 10 at every β (sync, largest GitHub); desync unchanged at β = 1 (≤ 0.002) and positive on all 10 at every β (+0.002 Facebook to +0.033 at β = 100). Async scan, f = 0.1: +0.033 to +0.076 on all 10 at β = 1 and 10 (median 95% and 77% of f = 1), at β = 100 all but Facebook (−0.001; +0.044 to +0.070 on the other 9). One at a time, A ≤ 1: +0.039 to +0.077 at β = 1, +0.022 to +0.080 at β = 100 on all 8 (Facebook +0.022 on these cells). A > 1, f = 0.1: −0.014 to −0.034 at β = 10 and −0.020 to −0.041 at β = 100 on all 10 (sync −0.004 to +0.005; desync −0.017 to −0.036); one at a time −0.018 to −0.041 at β = 100.
  - real − degseq: |diff| ≤ 0.011 at β ≤ 1 on all 10 (sync; desync at β = 1 ≤ 0.008; async scan f = 0.1 ≤ 0.014). High β, sync: co-authorship networks +0.040 (astroph) to +0.062 (grqc), Facebook up to +0.024, others −0.011 to +0.016, asinternet −0.027. Desync at β = 100: ≥ 0.01 only on grqc +0.028, astroph +0.017, Facebook +0.012; asinternet −0.010. Async scan, β = 100, f = 0.1: grqc +0.007 (f = 1: +0.082); negative on Facebook −0.032, astroph −0.023, emaileu −0.015; asinternet and GitHub +0.010. The high-β co-authorship effect was a lockstep effect. What remains without lockstep is small and has no consistent sign: f = 0.1, β ≥ 10: −0.032 to +0.019; one at a time: −0.010 to +0.016 (A ≤ 1); A > 1: within ±0.006 at every f.
  - Clustering (WS − ER): ≈ 0 at β ≤ 1 (≥ −0.008). High β, sync: +0.011 to +0.070 (gnutella ≈ 0), largest on grqc and astroph. Desync at β = 100: ≥ 0.01 only on grqc +0.029, astroph +0.025, Facebook +0.013 (the three most clustered networks); others +0.009 or less. Extra scans, f = 0.1, β = 100: |WS − ER| < 0.01 on 7 of 10. The three exceptions all come from a = 0.2 cells, where WS and ER settle in different states, not from a general clustering effect: grqc +0.045 ((1.5, 0.2): WS 0.473, ER 0.334; other cells +0.006 to +0.025; also still falling with f: f = 1 +0.132, 0.5 +0.075, 0.25 +0.054; one at a time +0.009 on its 2 cells), Facebook −0.061 ((1.5, 0.2): ER 0.484, WS 0.231), asinternet −0.032 ((1.5, 0.2) −0.074, (2.0, 0.2) −0.047). β = 1: within ±0.01 except asinternet (−0.010 to −0.014). A > 1: |WS − ER| ≤ 0.010 at f ≤ 0.5. So the high-β clustering benefit is lockstep.
  - Fits Cimini & Sánchez 2014, Amaral et al. 2016 (RL largely insensitive to topology), now at every β once updating is not in lockstep (only degrees have a consistent effect).
  - Not "shuffling doesn't matter": shuffled seats do matter through degree (C2). Rewiring links at fixed degrees barely does.
  - ER/BA/WS = one graph each: CIs hold run noise only.

### Hypotheses (written 2026-10-12, before the sweep)

One dry-run cell was seen before writing: grqc, β = 10, a = 0.4, b = 1.5, one rep: real 0.54, degseq 0.45, shuffled 0.40.

- **H1 (C1).** A ≤ 1: Spearman(cooperation, degree) < 0 on all 5. Weakest where a hub's neighbourhood is least like the population: grqc (hubs next to hubs). Reverses at A > 1, β ≥ 3 as before.
- **H2 (C2).** real − shuffled > 0 in A ≤ 1 on all 5 and grows with β.
- **H3 (C3).** |real − degseq| ≤ 0.01 at β ≤ 1 on all 5. At high β it may exceed experiment 1's 0.024 on grqc and astroph, where rewiring removes the most structure (clustering → ~0.01, grqc modularity 0.85 → 0.37).

Added 2026-10-13 (ER/BA/WS for all 10 networks), before those runs:

- **H4 (C3, degree spread).** A ≤ 1: degseq − ER > 0 on all 10 networks, largest where degrees are most uneven (asinternet, GitHub). Basis: partial sweep v2 data (`../Experimentation`, 4 networks, A ≤ 1) gave +0.02 to +0.05; already seen, so a replication, not a blind prediction.

(a) **holds**: f = 1 equals `results/validate_bm.csv` (|diff| ≤ 1e-15, summation order only). (b) **fails**: static − shuffled at k = 2, b/c = 6, first 25/f rounds: +0.084 (f = 1) → +0.067 → +0.055 → +0.050 (f = 0.1) → +0.047 (f = 0.05; 95% CI ±0.002 from a 200-run estimate), i.e. 60% kept at f = 0.1, not ≤ 50%. Their pattern survives at every f (k = 4: +0.042 → +0.028; k = 6: +0.020 → +0.016; k = 8: +0.015 → +0.011). (c) **holds**: b/c = 2 within 0.007 at every f. Later in the run (rounds 25/f–50/f) the gap falls much more (k = 2: +0.080 → +0.021 at f = 0.05). So the paper must not say synchrony explains their result: on rings network reciprocity partly survives without lockstep within their window and decays later; our real-network setting differs in game, aspiration, β, degree and time window.

## Reporting rules

- Per network and regime, never pooled over networks (opposite signs cancel).
- Smallest effect worth reporting: |difference| ≥ 0.01 cooperation. Many CIs (networks × regimes × β × contrasts): read patterns across β, not single CIs.
- Dead zone (a ≥ 0.8) and β = 0.01 (not settled) flagged, not used for claims.
- 95% CI unit: rep (n = 10); with degseq involved, the rewiring (n = 5).
