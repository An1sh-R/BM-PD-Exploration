ASYNC SCAN: REPORT
2026-10-23

The desync check (each player updates with probability 0.5) shrank most high-beta effects,
but it changed two things at once: it broke the lockstep and it halved each player's
updates. The async scan separates them. Result: the drop comes from breaking the lockstep.
With no lockstep the fixed-partner effect is about 0, and at beta >= 10 slightly negative.
Hub defection (C1), keeping one's degree at low beta and the benefit of uneven degrees
survive. Main regime throughout: A <= 1 (a cooperator can be satisfied).

Every number below was recomputed from results/async_scan_runs.csv.


1. WHAT WAS RUN
---------------

Mentor's script (src/async_scan.py). update_fraction f = 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1: each round a
player updates its cooperation probability with probability f; everyone still plays every
round. Runs last 1,500/f rounds (window = last 500/f), so every player gets about 1,500
updates at every f. A smaller f removes the lockstep, not learning time. f = 0.1 = about
one player in ten updates per round.

Grid: 10 networks x 5 graph types (real, degseq, samedeg, shuffled, ER) x f {1, 0.5,
0.25, 0.1} x beta {1, 10, 100} x a {0.2, 0.4} x b {1.5, 2.0} x 5 reps = 12,000 runs,
all with A <= 1. 154 min on 12 processes. Same seeds as the earlier sweeps.

Changes made to the script before any run (no extra runs):
  - Node cooperation (for the hub-defection rho) is sampled every 1/f rounds, so every
    player contributes about 500 actions at every f. Averaging all 500/f window rounds
    would give 10x less noise at f = 0.1, and less noise alone makes rho look stronger.
    Measurement only (fast_model.run_bm record_every); no rule change.
  - Extra columns: drift, mean_p (did the long runs settle) and cooperation over rounds
    1,000-1,500 (to compare with the desync sweep).

Checks:
  - Before launch: 20/20 unit tests; numpy vs Mesa model 13/13 cells; 30 old runs and 6
    node files reproduced bit-identically with the new sampling option.
  - f = 1 equals the synchronous sweeps bit-identically: 3,000 of 3,000 runs
    (cooperation, drift, oscillation gap and mean p).
  - f = 0.5, rounds 1,000-1,500, equals the desync sweep bit-identically: 3,000 of 3,000.
  - Settling: |drift| > 0.01 in 0.3% of runs at f = 0.5, 0.2% at f = 0.25, 0.3% at f = 0.1
    (worst: emaileu degseq, beta = 100). Period-2 oscillation (gap > 0.01): 15% of runs at
    f = 1, 5.5% at f = 0.5, none at f <= 0.25.

Contrasts: per network, mean over the 4 (b, a) cells, 95% CI over the 5 reps (for degseq
each rep is its own rewiring). Share kept = value at f / value at f = 1 per network,
where |value at f = 1| >= 0.005; medians over networks. The f = 1 values are over this
scan's 4 cells only, so they differ from the full-regime sync tables in README.md.

Mean cooperation (all 10 networks):

  graph type  beta   f = 1   0.5    0.25   0.1
  real          1    0.387  0.379  0.376  0.374
  real         10    0.460  0.402  0.383  0.374
  real        100    0.472  0.407  0.384  0.373
  samedeg     100    0.423  0.386  0.384  0.384
  shuffled    100    0.401  0.381  0.381  0.381
  ER          100    0.400  0.347  0.334  0.328

The rise of cooperation with beta on real networks is itself a lockstep effect: at
f = 0.1 real cooperation is 0.373-0.374 at every beta.


2. H9: PREDICTION VS OUTCOME
----------------------------

H9 was written before the runs (ResearchQ/question.md).

(a) Checks: f = 1 reproduces sync, f = 0.5 reproduces desync. HOLDS (both 3,000/3,000,
    bit-identical).
(b) Fixed partners (real - samedeg) shrink towards 0 as f falls, at beta = 10 and 100.
    HOLDS, more strongly than predicted: about 0 by f = 0.25 and slightly negative at
    f = 0.1. It also shrinks at beta = 1.
(c) Hub defection and the degree effects show no trend towards 0. HOLDS for hub defection
    and degree spread (exception: Facebook at beta = 100). Keeping one's degree: no change
    at beta = 1; at beta >= 10 most of its sync size goes by f = 0.5, then flat.
    Not predicted: structure beyond degree (real - degseq) at high beta is mostly
    lockstep too.
(d) If (b) holds at equal updates, the desync drop comes from the lockstep, not from fewer
    updates. HOLDS: at f = 0.5 the partner effect is the same after about 750 updates
    (rounds 1,000-1,500 = the desync sweep) and about 1,500 updates (the scan's window):
    at most 0.006 apart on every network and beta.

---------------------

  100    -0.029 to +0.131        26%           4%   -14%    -0.019 to +0.004

At f = 0.1, beta = 100 (f = 1 value in brackets):
  negative, CIs exclude 0: deezer -0.019 (+0.058), gnutella -0.019 (+0.034), lastfm
    -0.019 (+0.064), twitch -0.018 (+0.030), astroph -0.016 (+0.084), emaileu -0.013
    (-0.029), grqc -0.012 (+0.131)
  about 0: asinternet +0.004 (+0.023), GitHub +0.003 (+0.015), Facebook -0.002 (+0.083)
At beta = 10, f = 0.1: -0.012 to -0.017 on astroph, deezer, gnutella, grqc, lastfm,
twitch; emaileu -0.007, Facebook -0.006; asinternet and GitHub 0.000.

CIs at f = 0.1 are 0.0001-0.0007 for the negative values (largest: GitHub 0.008).

Keeping one's degree (samedeg - shuffled):
  - beta = 1: identical at every f (asinternet +0.039, GitHub +0.033, Twitch +0.021,
    emaileu +0.014, others +0.001 to +0.008).
  - beta >= 10: sync up to +0.093 (emaileu, beta = 100); 11-20% kept (median) at f = 0.1,
    at most +0.011.

Fixed seats overall (real - shuffled), f = 0.1: at beta = 1 still +0.010 to +0.039 on 6
of 10 (asinternet +0.039, GitHub +0.039, Twitch +0.025, emaileu +0.019, Facebook +0.014,
astroph +0.011), mostly from keeping one's degree. At beta = 10 and 100, -0.025 to +0.009
(about -0.01 to -0.02 on 6-7 networks).

So the earlier statement "fixed partners help, more so at high beta" holds only with
synchronous updating. Why fixed partners slightly lower cooperation without lockstep has
not been studied.

Structure beyond the degree sequence (real - degseq):
  - beta = 1: within -0.010 to +0.014 at every f.
  - beta = 100: median kept 44% (f = 0.5), 23% (0.25), 8% (0.1). grqc +0.082 -> +0.007,

Clustering (WS - ER) is not in the scan. Under desync it kept >= 0.01 only on grqc,
astroph and Facebook. Since real - degseq on those three vanishes or turns negative by
f = 0.1, the remaining clustering effect is probably lockstep too. Untested.

6. CLAIM REVISIONS (applied 2026-10-23 in ResearchQ/question.md and README.md)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

C1  Unchanged in substance; now "robust to desync and to the async scan". Adds the
    f = 0.1 ranges and Facebook at beta = 100 as the exception.
C2  Before: fixed partners help on all 10 networks, but how much depends on synchronous
    updating.
    Now: fixed partners help only when players update in lockstep (about 0 at beta = 1,
    slightly negative at beta >= 10 once the lockstep is removed). Keeping one's degree
    helps at low beta, robustly (asinternet, GitHub, Twitch, emaileu). The open question
    "lockstep or fewer updates?" is answered: lockstep.
C3  Before: uneven degrees help, robustly; at high beta clustering adds cooperation only on
    the most clustered networks.
    Now: uneven degrees help, robustly. Beyond the degree sequence, structure barely
    matters once players do not update in lockstep (real - degseq at high beta mostly
    lockstep; clustering untested here).


7. CAVEATS
----------

- A <= 1 only, 4 (b, a) cells. A > 1 (where the hub reversal and the negative partner
  effect under desync were seen) is not in the scan.
- WS (clustering), BA and beta = 3 are not in the grid.
- beta = 1 has 4 cells x 5 reps; small effects there (<= 0.007) are near the reporting
  threshold of 0.01.
- ER is one graph per network: its CIs hold run noise only.
- f = 0.1 is still not one-player-at-a-time (about 10% of players update together each
  round). The trends from f = 0.5 to 0.1 are smooth, so a fully sequential run would most
  likely push further in the same direction.
